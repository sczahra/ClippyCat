## Source selection

Both attempts contain the intended eight-frame, row-major Scratch sequence and are visually very similar. The chosen original candidate is the closer and safer recovery source: its Marmalade identity, anatomy, and scratch motion are at least as clean as the alternate, and its darker, higher-contrast checkerboard is farther from the cat's warm white and orange fur. The alternate uses a lighter checker that is closer to white fur and therefore carries more color-keying risk.

## Checkerboard observed

- The background is a baked neutral blue-gray checker with small generated color variation rather than two perfectly flat colors.
- Light squares are centered approximately around RGB `(195, 197, 201)`; observed common values span roughly `(193-197, 194-199, 199-203)`.
- Dark squares are centered approximately around RGB `(136, 139, 143)`; observed common values span roughly `(134-138, 136-141, 140-145)`.
- Checker squares are approximately 14 x 14 pixels, with an approximately 28-pixel full repeating period.

## Cleanup method

1. Divide the source into its existing 4-column by 2-row panel layout using proportional boundaries, preserving row-major frame order.
2. Classify only neutral, blue-gray pixels close to the known checker families as background candidates.
3. Keep only candidate regions connected to each panel edge, so similar colors enclosed within Marmalade are not erased globally.
4. Derive the cat silhouette from the largest connected non-background component in each panel, avoiding isolated checker remnants.
5. Apply a narrow edge-aware alpha transition and matte-color cleanup only along the recovered silhouette boundary. Warm white/orange fur, markings, eyes, paws, whiskers, and interior pixels will not be globally color-keyed.
6. Inspect a transparency review sheet before installing the recovered files. If the cleanup visibly damages fur, paws, whiskers, or white markings, stop without installing the pack.

## Frame segmentation and placement

- Extract exactly eight panels in row-major order: four across the top row, then four across the bottom row.
- Preserve the existing motion order: seated setup, leg lift, reach, contact A, contact B, contact A, leg lower, recovery.
- Use one common scale factor for all eight silhouettes so the largest frame fits safely within a 192 x 208 canvas.
- Center each frame horizontally and align its visible baseline consistently near the bottom of the canvas, leaving a safety margin so ears, paws, tail, fur, and whiskers remain inside the cell.
- Write final RGBA frames only to `Assets/actions/Marmalade/scratch/0.png` through `7.png`.
- Write the numbered transparency review sheet only to `codex-checkpoints/action-previews/marmalade-scratch-recovered.png`.

## Risks

- The source was rendered over the checker, so anti-aliased fur edges contain some checker color and cannot be recovered as perfectly as native transparent artwork.
- Fine whiskers and isolated fur tips are the most vulnerable details; conservative connected-region masking may retain a faint matte or omit a very weak edge pixel.
- The generated panels are not native 192 x 208 cells, so uniform downscaling is required. No pose-specific redrawing, silhouette modification, or creative correction will be performed.
- A hard geometric split could capture panel padding inconsistently; content will therefore be measured after cleanup and placed with one shared scale and baseline rule.

## Explicitly out of scope

- Regenerating, redrawing, restyling, or creatively editing Scratch artwork
- Merry Scratch or any Yawn artwork
- `Program.cs`, current sprite atlases, `actions.json`, importer scripts, project files, or runtime behavior
- Manifest updates, importer execution, app wiring, packages, shadows, backgrounds, text, borders, or unrelated cleanup
